# Main Model Outputs (GDPR Compliance Assessments)

This directory contains the raw JSON data generated by the primary Large Language Models (**GPT-5.2** and **Grok**) during the experimental phase of the thesis. These outputs represent the models' direct assessments of the Ada Health GmbH privacy policy against the seven principles of GDPR Article 5.

## Overview

The experiments were conducted in multiple phases to test the impact of prompt engineering on legal reasoning stability and accuracy:

- **Zero-Shot Strategy**: The models assessed the policy without any preceding examples, relying on their internal training and the specific instructions provided in the prompt.
- **Few-Shot Strategy**: The models were provided with 4 annotated examples (sourced from the Claudette project) to guide their reasoning logic and output structure before analyzing the Ada policy.

## Directory Structure

The outputs are organized hierarchically by model and then by prompting strategy:

- **`gpt5.2/`**: Raw outputs from OpenAI's GPT-5.2.
  - **`zero_shot/`**: Baseline and structured zero-shot evaluations.
  - **`few_shot/`**: Results from the graduated few-shot versions, including runs with **Extended Thinking** mode enabled.
- **`grok/`**: Raw outputs from xAI's Grok.
  - **`zero_shot/`**: Baseline and structured zero-shot evaluations.
  - **`few_shot/`**: Results from the graduated few-shot versions, including runs with **Expert Mode** enabled.

## Contents of JSON Files

Each JSON file follows a strict schema defined in the prompts to facilitate automated parsing:
- `principle`: The specific GDPR Article 5 principle being assessed.
- `article_5_text`: The model's reproduction of the official legal text.
- `policy_clauses`: Verbatim quotes and section numbers from the Ada Health policy.
- `rating`: The compliance label (**Compliant**, **Partially Compliant**, or **Non-compliant**).
- `justification`: A structured legal reasoning connecting the policy text to the GDPR requirements.

## Analysis Script

The `analyze_gdpr_outputs.py` script located in the project root (or within this directory in some structures) was used to process these files, check for schema completeness, and generate the master comparison tables used in the thesis findings.
